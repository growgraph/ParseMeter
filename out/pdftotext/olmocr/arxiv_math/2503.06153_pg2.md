When r = 1, M(1, L, c2 ) is isomorphic to S [c2 ] , the Hilbert scheme of 0-dimensional
closed subschemes in S of length c2 . Fogarty showed that S [n] (n ∈ Z≥0 ) is a smooth
projective variety of dimension 2n [Fog68] and computed its Picard group [Fog73]. The
Betti numbers of S [n] were completely determined by Göttsche [G9̈0]. When r ≥ 2,
M(r, L, c2 ) can be reducible or non-reduced. But starting from Donaldson’s generic
smoothness result [Don90], the study of M(r, L, c2 ) has revealed that M(r, L, c2 ) has
better geometric properties as we fix r ≥ 2, L, and let c2 increase. For example,
when c2 is large enough, Gieseker–Li [GL94] [GL96] and O’Grady [O’G96] showed
that M(r, L, c2 ) is irreducible, normal, and generically smooth of expected dimension.
Some asymptotic phenomena also occur in the Picard group and Betti numbers of
M(r, L, c2 ) (r ≥ 2) [Qin92][Li96][Li97][CW22].
This raises a natural question:
Question 1.1. Are there asymptotic phenomena similar to those above in the geometry
of M(r, L, c2 ) when r = 0?
Sheaves on S of rank zero are supported on points or curves. In this paper, we
focus on those pure sheaves supported on curves, which are called one-dimensional
sheaves. In contrast to the positive rank case, there are few general results for the
moduli space of one-dimensional sheaves. An essential difficulty is that the supports
of one-dimensional sheaves can be singular, even non-reduced. To distinguish this case,
we use the following notation. Given a nonzero effective divisor β on S and an integer
χ, denote by Mβ,χ the moduli space parametrizing polystable one-dimensional sheaves
F on S, with determinant det(F ) ∼
= OS (β) and Euler characteristic χ(F ) = χ. We
will make the following assumption on β, unless otherwise stated.
Assumption 1.2. The following conditions hold:
(1) β is base-point-free.
(2) A general curve in |β| is smooth and connected.
A conjecture [SZ24, Conjecture 1.3] was proposed on the stabilization of (intersection
cohomology) Betti numbers of Mβ,χ when χ is fixed and β is sufficiently positive, as an
analogue of [CW22, Conjecture 1.1]. When S is a del Pezzo surface, this conjecture was
resolved in [PSSZ24] by proving an asymptotic formula of the refined BPS invariants
which also refine the Betti numbers.
The primary motivation of this paper is to explore the stabilization of the Picard
number ρ(Mβ,χ ) of Mβ,χ when β is sufficiently positive. Here, the Picard number ρ(X)
of a proper scheme X is defined as the dimension of the Q-vector space NumQ (X) of
the numerically equivalence classes of line bundles on X with rational coefficients, in
the sense of [Kle66]. Fix a basis [Li ] (i = 1, · · · , ρ(S)) for NumQ (S), where each Li is a
smooth, connected effective divisor. We specify a positivity condition for β as follows.
Definition 1.3. We say a divisor β on S satisfies (P) if
(1) β satisfies Assumption 1.2.
(2) The arithmetic genus pa (β) > 0.
(3) For each 1 ≤ i ≤ ρ(S), there exists a smooth and connected curve L′i ∈ |β − Li |
such that Li meets L′i transversally with intersection number Li · L′i ≥ |χ| + 1.
Note that for an ample divisor β, the multiple nβ satisfies (P) for all n ≫ 0.
Our first main result is the following. We obtain an asymptotic lower bound of the
Picard number of Mβ,χ .
