Lemma 2.3. There exists $m(u)\in\mathbb{N}$ such that, for all $m\geq m(u)$ ,for all $m\geq s,r\geq$ _bum with $s\neq r$ the probability that a particle v in location $V(v)$ with $\pi_{m}(V(v))=r$ has an offspring y with location $V(y)$ satisfying $\pi_{m}(V(y))=s$ is at most 

$$\beta(r\wedge s)^{-\gamma}(r\vee s)^{\gamma-1}.$$

Proof. For a fixed particle v in location $V(v)$ with $\pi_{m}(V(v))=r$ the probability that it has an offspring y with location $V(y)$ satisfying $\pi_{m}(V(y))=s$ equals 

$$1-\exp\bigg(-\tilde{\pi}\Big(-\sum_{k=s}^{m}\frac{1}{k}-V(v),-\sum_{k=s+1}^{m}\frac{1}{k}-V(v)\Big]\bigg).$$

As $\pi_{m}(V(v))=r$ we have 

$$-\sum_{k=r}^{m}{\frac{1}{k}}<V(v)\leq-\sum_{k=r+1}^{m}{\frac{1}{k}}.$$

The probability in $(4)$ is therefore largest when $\begin{array}{r}{V(v)=-\sum_{k=r}^{m}\frac{1}{k}}\end{array}$ . It therefore remains to show that, for bum $\leq s<r$ , we have 



$$1-\exp\bigg(-\tilde{\pi}\Big(-\sum_{k=s}^{r-1}\frac{1}{k},-\sum_{k=s+1}^{r-1}\frac{1}{k}\Big]\bigg)\leq\beta s^{-\gamma}r^{\gamma-1},$$

and, for bum $\leq r<s$ , we have 

$$1-\exp\bigg(-\tilde{\pi}\Big(\sum_{k=r}^{s-1}\frac{1}{k},\sum_{k=r}^{s}\frac{1}{k}\Big]\bigg)\leq\beta s^{\gamma-1}r^{-\gamma}.$$

For (5) we find that, for some constant $C>0$ ,if $m\geq m(u)$ for a suitable $m(u)\in\mathbb{N}$ 

$$\begin{align*}\Big[\tilde{\pi}\Big(-\sum_{k=s}^{r-1}\frac{1}{k},-\sum_{k=s+1}^{r-1}\frac{1}{k}\Big]&=\frac{\tilde{\beta}}{1-\gamma}\;\exp\Big(-(1-\gamma)\sum_{k=s}^{r-1}\frac{1}{k}\Big)(e^{\frac{1-\gamma}{s+1}}-1)\\&\leq\Big(\frac{\tilde{\beta}}{s+1}+\frac{C}{(bum)^2}\Big)\;\exp\big(-(1-\gamma)(\log\Big(\frac{r-1}{s-1}\Big)-\frac{C}{bum})\big)\\&\leq\beta s^{-\gamma}r^{\gamma-1}.\end{align*}$$
