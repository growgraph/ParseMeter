Claim 6.6. y lies in P(v ′ 1 , x′ 1 ).

Proof. If y does not lie in P(v ′ 1 , x′ 1 ), then y must lie in the path between v<sup>i</sup><sup>l</sup> and x ′ 1 (or the path between v<sup>i</sup><sup>l</sup> and v ′ 1 ). If it lies on the path between v<sup>i</sup><sup>l</sup> and x ′ 1 (resp. between v<sup>i</sup><sup>l</sup> and v ′ 1 ), then we have a path from v<sup>i</sup><sup>l</sup> to x ′ (resp. between v<sup>i</sup><sup>l</sup> and v ′ ) which has length smaller than d(v<sup>i</sup><sup>l</sup> , x′ ) (resp. d(v<sup>i</sup><sup>l</sup> , v′ )), which is a contradiction. Hence, y lies in P(v ′ 1 , x′ 1 ) (refer to Figure 12b).

Note that d(x ′ 1 , y) ≥ <sup>d</sup>(<sup>x</sup> 1 , v<sup>i</sup><sup>l</sup> ) and d(v ′ 1 , y) ≥ <sup>d</sup>(<sup>v</sup> ′ 1 , v<sup>i</sup><sup>l</sup> ). If not, then there exists a shorter or equal length path from v ′ to v<sup>i</sup><sup>l</sup> (from x ′ to v<sup>i</sup><sup>l</sup> ) that bypasses the edge v<sup>i</sup><sup>l</sup> v (resp. v<sup>i</sup>lx), which is a contradiction to the fact that the pair v<sup>i</sup><sup>l</sup> , v′ (resp. v<sup>i</sup><sup>l</sup> , x′ ) monitors the edge v<sup>i</sup><sup>l</sup> v (resp. v<sup>i</sup>lx). Hence d<sup>P</sup> (x ′ 1 , v′ 1 ) = d(x ′ 1 , y) + d(y, v′ 1 ) ≥ <sup>d</sup>(<sup>v</sup> ′ 1 , v<sup>i</sup><sup>l</sup> ) + d(x ′ 1 , v<sup>i</sup><sup>l</sup> ), implying d<sup>P</sup> (x ′ 1 , v′ 1 ) = d(v ′ 1 , v<sup>i</sup><sup>l</sup> ) + d(x ′ 1 , v<sup>i</sup><sup>l</sup> ). This implies that d(x 1 , y) = d(x ′ 1 , v<sup>i</sup><sup>l</sup> ) and d(v ′ 1 , y) = d(v ′ 1 , v<sup>i</sup><sup>l</sup> ), which implies that the vertex y is unique in P(v ′ 1 , x′ 1 ). This fact together with Claim 6.6 implies that in C, v<sup>i</sup><sup>l</sup> is incident to exactly one chord.

Now note that if v and y are not adjacent, then the induced 2-path yv<sup>i</sup><sup>l</sup> v is part of a chordless cycle of length at least 4, which is a contradiction. Hence vy ∈ <sup>E</sup>(Gk+1). Similarly, it can be shown that xy ∈ <sup>E</sup>(Gk+1). Hence vv<sup>i</sup>l<sup>x</sup> is part of a 4-cycle vv<sup>i</sup>lxyv, which leads to a contradiction. Hence v ′ and x ′ monitor vv<sup>i</sup><sup>l</sup> .

Hence, until now, we showed that all edges in E<sup>l</sup> can be monitored by the vertices of Man(Gk) \ {<sup>v</sup><sup>i</sup><sup>l</sup> }. Hence, combining this fact with Claim 6.1 and 6.3, we can conclude that Man(Gk+1) forms an optimal MEG set of <sup>G</sup>k+1. Hence, meg(G) = |Man(G)| for every strongly chordal graph G.
