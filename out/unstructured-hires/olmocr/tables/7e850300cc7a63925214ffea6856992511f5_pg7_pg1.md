The maximum adsorption capacities of MWCNTs and SWCNTs were found to be 2.83 and 2.4 mg/g, respective- ly. It was obvious that the MWCNT adsorbent was more effective in re- moving ﬂuoride from aqueous solutions. This could be due to the complex formation with MWCNTs as referenced earlier but a more like- ly explanation is the higher availability of internally available adsorp- tion sites on MWCNTs due to the different pore size diameters resulting in a larger internal available surface area than for SWCNTs [43]. The adsorption of ﬂuoride onto carbon is usually by physical ad- sorption and is mainly dependent on two parameters, the speciﬁc sur- face area and the pore size distribution. In the case of the two CNTs, the SWCNTs have a signiﬁcantly larger surface area of 700 m2/g but

have a slightly lower maximum ﬂuoride adsorption capacity of 2.4 mg/g. The pore entry diameter limits for the SWCNTs may account for the relatively low ﬂuoride adsorption capacity of this 700 m2/g material.
