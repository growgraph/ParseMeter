## CONCLUSIONS

From a theoretical analysis of radial blade temperatures near the coolant passage in liquid-cooled gas turbines and for the conditions used in this inveatigation, the following conclusions are drawn:

1. For a coolant flow of 6.42 pounds per second, the blade metal temperatures in the regions near the coolant passages may be kept as low as one-fifth, two-fifthe, and one-half the effective gas temperature for water, ethylene glycol, and kerosene coolants, respectively.

2. The temperature distributions for all blade lengths with coolant passage extending to within onə-sixteenth inch of the blade tips were found to show a rotor temperature about equal to the coolant temperature, a steady temperature rise through the rim and a small part of the blades, a nearly constant or prevalent blade temperature through most of the liquid-cooled part of the blades, and a aharp temperature rise to the blade tips.

3. An increase in the length of the coolant passagea in a $4 \frac { 1 } { 2 6 }$ 1nch blade results in extending the prevalont blade temperature over a greater blade distance; i.e., the longer the coolant passages, the longer is that portion of the blade whose tomperature is about one-fifth the effective gas temperature if water at a flow of 6.42 pounds per second is used as the coolant.

4. An increase in coolant flow from 2 to 15 pounds per second results in doubling the cooling effectivoness in the turbine blades.

5. The results of liquid cooling indirectly indicate that large increases in effective gas temperature are possible without the occurrence of metal failures compared to the smail increase of only $2 0 0 ^ { \circ } \mathbb { F }$ in effective gas temperature obtainable for most blades with rim cooling.
