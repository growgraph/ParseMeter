The physiological meaning of these findings is that, after inoculation, cells adapt to the new growth condition by the uniform and concerted acceleration of their specific growth rate and specific methane-forming activity in a way that $Y _ { \mathrm { C H _ { 4 } } }$ remains fixed.

The specific growth rates became maximal at the entry of the exponential stage and remained constant throughout the particular stage (Fig. 1B, 2B, and 3A). The values did not significantly differ among the various gassing regimens (0.22 to 0.25 $\mathrm { h } ^ { - 1 } ;$ doubling times of 2.8 to 3.1 h) (Table 3). However, the $q _ { \mathrm { C H _ { 4 } } }$ was not constant. Specific methane-forming activities initially increased to a maximum and declined thereafter. The decline occurred concomitant with the decrease in the dissolved hydrogen partial pressures, resulting from the increasing hydrogen uptake rates by the growing biomass (Fig. 1 and 2 and Table 3). Apparently, the physiology of the cells continuously changed, even at a constant growth rate.
