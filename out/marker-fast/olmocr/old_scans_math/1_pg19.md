$$\text{Let } \frac{x^2}{(x^2+1)(x^2+4)} = \frac{A}{(x^2+1)} + \frac{B}{(x^2+4)}$$

$$x^2 = A(x^2+4) + B(x^2+1).$$

Let  $x = \sqrt{-1}$ , or  $x^2 = -1$ ,

$$\therefore -1 = 3A, \quad \therefore A = -\frac{1}{3},$$

$$\therefore x^2 + \frac{1}{3}(x^2+4) = B(x^2+1),$$

$$\therefore \frac{4(x^2+1)}{3} = B(x^2+1), \quad \therefore B = \frac{4}{3}$$

$$\begin{aligned} \int \frac{x^2 dx}{(x^2+1)(x^2+4)} &= -\frac{1}{3} \int \left\{ \frac{dx}{(x^2+1)} - \int \frac{4dx}{(x^2+4)} \right\} \\ &= \frac{1}{3} \int \left\{ \frac{dx}{(1+x^2)} - \frac{dx}{\left(1+\frac{x^2}{4}\right)} \right\} \\ &= \frac{1}{3} \left\{ 2 \tan^{-1} \frac{x}{2} - \tan^{-1} x \right\}. \end{aligned}$$
