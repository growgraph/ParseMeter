But the emptiness of the regions T (3, 2, k3) for k<sup>3</sup> ⩾ 3 follows from lemma 3(3c). Therefore" A◦ 3 (3, 1, 2) consists of the triples (23).

Further, if (k1, k2, k3) ∈ A<sup>∗</sup> 3 (3, 1, 2), then we necessarily have (k1, k2) ∈ A<sup>∗</sup> 2 (3, 1, 2). As it was stated before, we either have k<sup>1</sup> ≡ 0 (mod 3), k<sup>1</sup> ⩾ 3, k<sup>2</sup> = 1, or k<sup>1</sup> = 3, k<sup>2</sup> = 2.

For k<sup>2</sup> = 1, the condition (19) corresponding to i = r = 3, reduces to

$$k_1(k_3 - 1) \not\equiv 0 \pmod{3}.$$

Since k<sup>1</sup> ≡ 0 (mod 3) in the first case, this condition can not be satisfied for any k3. In the second case, the same condition takes the form

$$7k_3 \not\equiv 0 \pmod{3}.$$

For k<sup>3</sup> ⩾ 3, the emptiness of T (3, 2, k3) follows from lemma 3 (3c). Therefore, non-empty regions correspond to the values k<sup>3</sup> = 1, k<sup>3</sup> = 2 only. Hence, the set A<sup>∗</sup> 3 (3, 1, 2) consists of the triples

(3, 2, 1), (3, 2, 2). (24)

It turns out that for any r ⩾ 4 the set A<sup>∗</sup> r (3, 1, 2) consists of two sets: (3, 2 r−2 , 1) and (3, 2 r−1 ). Let us prove it by induction with respect to r.

For r = 3, it was proved earlier. Suppose now that this assertion holds for any r, 3 ⩽ r ⩽ m − 1, and check it for r = m.

Indeed, let (k1, . . . , km) ∈ A<sup>∗</sup> <sup>m</sup>(3, 1, 2). Then (k1, . . . , km−1) ∈ A<sup>∗</sup> m−1 (3, 1, 2), so, by the induction, (k1, . . . , km−1) is either (3, 2 m−3 , 1) or (3, 2 m−2 ). Hence, (k1, . . . , km) has the form (3, 2 m−3 , 1, k) or (3, 2 m−2 , k), where k ⩾ 1 is some integer.

In the first case, the condition (19) corresponding to i = m, has the form

| $\mathbb{K}_m(3, 2^{m-3}, 1, k) + \mathbb{K}_{m-1}(2^{m-3}, 1, k) \not\equiv 2 \pmod{3}.$ | (25) |
|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------|
|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------|

By explicit formulas for the continuants, we get

$$\mathbb{K}_m(3, 2^{m-3}, 1, k) = 2(k - m) + 3, \quad \mathbb{K}_{m-1}(2^{m-3}, 1, k) = k - m + 2,$$

so (25) has the form 3(k − m) + 5 ̸≡ 2 (mod 3). Therefore, it is not satisfied for any k.

In the second case, we have the condition

$$\mathbb{K}_m(3, 2^{m-2}, k) + \mathbb{K}_{m-1}(2^{m-2}, k) \not\equiv 2 \pmod{3}.$$

Using the formulas

$$\mathbb{K}_m(3, 2^{m-2}, k) = 2km - k - 2m + 3, \quad \mathbb{K}_m(2^{m-2}, k) = km - k - m + 2,$$

we rewrite it as follows

$$3m(k-1) - 2k + 5 \not\equiv 2 \pmod{3}, \quad \text{that is, } k \not\equiv 0 \pmod{3}.$$
